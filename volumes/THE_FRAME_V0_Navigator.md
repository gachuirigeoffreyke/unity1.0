## 2. How THE FRAME works

**Three layers, each with one job.**

- **The wall tells you what to do today.** A few A3 wall sheets (W1–W10, at most four up at once) hang where the action happens.
- **The workbook holds the numbers.** Daily inputs, weekly averages, the calorie action, test results.
- **The volumes hold the reasoning.** Why each rule exists, what to do when things go wrong, and the evidence behind it.

**Reading order.** Read V0 and V1 once. Live in the volume for your current phase (V2 now). Open V5–V9 when their topic comes up.

**Three columns, always.** Every exercise row has three versions:

- **Bodyweight:** your body plus floor, wall, door frame, stairs, or a sturdy chair or table.
- **Intermediary:** household items, such as a backpack loaded with 2 kg unga or sugar packets, jerrycans, a towel, a bedsheet shut in a door, or a bicycle inner tube.
- **Ideal:** the kit, meaning pull-up bar, bands, dumbbells and rings.

Use the column you can set up that day. Sets, reps and effort don't change.

**Changes go through the gates.** A missed week, illness or travel uses the re-entry rules in V4, not a restart. Decisions are logged in the change log of the volume they touch.

**Busy stretches have a floor.** If study or work crunches, drop to the maintenance minimum: one hard session a week, one set per exercise, same effort. In younger adults, that dose has held strength and muscle size for up to 32 weeks, as long as intensity stays up ([Spiering et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33629972/)). V4 turns this into a protocol.

**Why the phases differ.**

- **Gain is driven by volume.** In volume-equated trials, periodising volume and intensity improved 1RM strength but did not change muscle growth ([Moesgaard et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/)). So Gain pushes hard sets, and Strength is where the loading gets periodised.
- **The Gain surplus is small.** Reviews of off-season practice suggest gaining about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight a week for novice and intermediate lifters, from a surplus of roughly 10–20% ([Iraki et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31247944/)). At 74.5 kg that is 0.19–0.37 kg a week. Gain starts at the low end so you stay close to Day-120 sharpness.

**Seasons.** The Build starts in the short rains (have the rain plan ready for Engine days) and peaks in the hot, dry season of January and February. Gain covers the long rains, so its sessions are indoor-proof. Strength lands in the cool, dry July–August months.

## 6. The weekly operating loop

**Seven days, one loop: Reset on Saturday, four lifting days, two engine days.** W1 carries the wall version.

| Day | Session | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sat | Reset + review (tests first on test days) | 45–60 min |
| Sun | Upper A | 60–75 min |
| Mon | Lower A | 60–75 min |
| Tue | Engine + Armour | 45–60 min |
| Wed | Upper B | 60–75 min |
| Thu | Lower B | 60–75 min |
| Fri | Engine + Armour | 45–60 min |

### Every day

- Weigh after waking, before food.
- 150 g of protein across four feedings of 30–41 g.
- 8,000+ steps.
- A movement snack after every 45–60 minutes of study.
- Lift 3–6 hours after the main study block.
- Caffeine off 8 hours before bed.
- Hard training done 90 minutes before bed.
- 7.5–8.5 hours in bed, with the same wake time every day.

### The Saturday check-in

1. Enter your waist on the Weekly sheet. Read the 7-day average, Status and Action. Calories change by 150–200 kcal only after two weeks outside the band.
2. Count the sessions done and note any pain scores.
3. Look at W1. If a deload or test week is coming, move the deload to meet a study crunch if needed, and log the move.
4. Plan the week's sessions around your study blocks.
5. Take the long walk with walk-and-recall.

## 10. Glossary

- **Annual Loop.** The repeating year from Year 2: Build & Sharpen (Nov–Feb), Reset (late Feb–Mar), Gain (Mar–Jul), Strength (Jul–Sep), Consolidate and review (Sep–Oct).
- **Band.** The weekly weight-change range your lane allows, read from the 7-day average.
- **Big 3.** Curl-up, side plank and bird dog: trunk-endurance work for the back.
- **Control loop.** Measure the trend, compare it with the band, adjust by 150–200 kcal only after two weeks off-band.
- **Deload.** A lighter week, about half the volume at RIR 4, every sixth week.
- **Double progression.** Hit the top of the rep range on every set, then add load or climb a rung.
- **Gate.** A dated decision point with set inputs, rules and a default.
- **Kneecap-joint (patellofemoral) pain.** Front-of-knee pain made worse by deep bending under load and long sitting. Handled with hip and knee strengthening at pain-free depth.
- **Lane.** Your calorie track (A lean gain, B recomp, C lean-out), set by the Day-1 waist.
- **Maintenance minimum.** The floor for busy stretches: one hard session a week, one set per exercise, same effort.
- **Mini-cut.** Four weeks about 300 kcal below lane calories, Weeks 14–17, to finish sharp.
- **Movement snack.** 3–5 minutes of walking and simple moves after each 45–60 minutes of study.
- **Recomp.** Building muscle while losing fat at about maintenance calories.
- **Red flags.** Pain below the knee, numbness, weakness or night pain mean a doctor or physio. Groin numbness or a new bladder or bowel change means emergency care.
- **Reset day / Reset phase.** The Saturday session (walk, check-in, planning), and the two maintenance weeks after Gate 1.
- **RIR (reps in reserve).** How many clean reps you had left at the end of a set.
- **Rung and ladder.** One step, and the full sequence, of a movement from easiest to hardest.
- **Three columns.** Bodyweight, Intermediary and Ideal versions of every exercise row.
- **Walk-and-recall.** Explaining the day's study material from memory during a walk.
- **Zone 2.** Easy aerobic work where you can still talk in full sentences.
