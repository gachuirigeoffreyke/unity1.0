# Mvuke · Volume 11 · Group, Merger & Shop

## Summary

The target is Mvuke and Planties under one holding company by about 2028, 2030 at the latest, sharing a Nairobi shop with a tea bar. The structure question is settled before Planties registers its company in November 2026. This volume is research, not legal or tax advice.

## Structure options

| Option | How it works | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A · Two companies now, holding company later | Each business registers on its own; around 2028 the founders swap their shares for holding company shares | No delay for Planties; each partner keeps a clean stake in their own business | The later swap may carry tax and stamp duty (R14.2) |
| B · Holding company first | A holding company is formed now and owns both businesses from the start | The cleanest group from day one; no later swap | Three companies to run from the start; every founder must agree ownership splits now; the land caveat may reach holding company shares (R14.3) |
| C · One company, two brands | One company runs Planties and Mvuke as brands | Cheapest to run | Mixes two partner groups and their risks; ruled out by the decision for separate entities |

Provisional lean: option A. Register Planties as planned, and put a clause in both shareholders' agreements committing everyone to consider a move into a holding company on agreed terms. Switch to option B only if the accountant shows that adding the holding company later is expensive.

> The conversation 1. Mjenzi and the Mvuke partner, this week, before the founders' memo is signed. 2. Mjenzi and the Planties partner. 3. All three together, once both have said yes in principle. 4. An advocate and an accountant, before Planties registers in November. Agenda: why a group (a shared shop, a brand family, shared suppliers and systems, a stronger story for investors); what does not change (each company keeps its founders, books and decisions); how ownership works (a share swap at agreed values, so nobody gives up their stake for free); timing (about 2028, 2030 at the latest); what happens if someone says no (separate companies, with the shop shared through a lease or joint venture); and Mjenzi's conflict (deals between the businesses need the other partner's approval). Ask each person for yes in principle, not yet, or no, with their concerns. No numbers yet. Related-party rules Every deal between Mvuke and Planties (plants in boxes, the referral commission, shared delivery, later shop rent) is written down with its price. The other business's partner approves it, since Mjenzi sits on both sides. Prices are what an outside party would pay, and both decisions logs record each deal. Shared shop (year 2) A Nairobi plant-and-wellness shop with retail, a tea bar and occasional workshops. Mjenzi designs the fit-out; the Mvuke partner leads the tea bar. Production stays at Limuru–Kiambu, possibly with a farm shop. The two companies share it through a lease and sub-lease, a joint venture or a licence (R15.5). The tea bar brings food premises licences and Data Commissioner registration, which bars and restaurants need whatever their size (R15.2). A local precedent: Melvins Teas opened a tea house in Lavington Mall. Go or no-go at Gate 3, mid-2028 (Volume 2).
