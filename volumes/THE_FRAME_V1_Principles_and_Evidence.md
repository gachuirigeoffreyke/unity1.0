# THE FRAME Playbook · V1 · Principles & Evidence

**Eight laws run THE FRAME.** Each comes from the research below, and each tells you what to do when a set, a week or a phase goes off-script. Where the evidence is thin, this volume says so.

Read it once now. Come back when a rule seems odd, or when a gate decision needs a reason.

## 1. How to read this volume

**Every law follows the same pattern.** You get the rule, the evidence behind it, what it means for your sessions, and a grade for how sure we are.

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Strong** | Several meta-analyses or a large meta-analysis agree. |
| **Moderate** | One meta-analysis, or several with real caveats. |
| **Practice** | A coaching convention or a THE FRAME rule, reasoned from evidence but not directly tested. |

## 2. The eight laws at a glance

| # | Law | THE FRAME rule | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 8 | Train for the phase | Gain is volume-led; Strength uses heavier, periodised loads | Strong |

## 3. The laws in detail

### Law 1 · Effort decides growth

**Rule.** Most working sets end 1–3 reps short of failure (RIR 1–3). Hinges, split squats and anything that loads the knee or back stay at RIR 2 or more.

**Evidence.**

- **Effort drives muscle growth more than strength.** In a large series of meta-regressions, muscle growth improved as sets ended closer to failure. Strength gains barely depended on it; load mattered more for strength ([Robinson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)).
- **The proximity figures are estimates.** The authors assigned RIR values to each study, so the exact numbers are approximate.
- **Effort matters most at light loads.** In a trial at 30% and 80% of one-rep max (the most you can lift once), going to failure added growth at the light load but nothing at the heavy load ([Lasevicius et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31895290/)).

**What it means for you.**

- In the Bodyweight and Intermediary columns, loads are light. Take safe moves such as push-ups, rows, curls and calf raises close to failure, at RIR 0–1.
- With heavier kit, RIR 1–3 is enough.
- Moves that load the spine or knee never go below RIR 2. The small extra stimulus isn't worth the risk.

**Grade.** Moderate.

### Law 2 · Load is flexible, which is why the three columns work

**Rule.** The Bodyweight, Intermediary and Ideal versions all build muscle when the set is hard. Once a set passes 30 reps, climb a rung.

**Evidence.**

- **Light and heavy loads grow muscle about equally.** When sets went to failure, loads at or below 60% of one-rep max grew muscle about as well as heavier loads. Heavy loads built more one-rep-max strength ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)).
- **This held in trained men.** Sets of 25–35 reps grew muscle comparably to sets of 8–12, while heavier loads won for strength ([Schoenfeld et al., 2015](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4037869?lng=en)).
- **Progressive push-ups matched bench pressing for strength.** In a 4-week trial, they raised bench-press strength as much as bench pressing did ([Kotarsky et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/)). Muscle thickness didn't change in either group over so short a trial, so this is strength evidence, not size evidence.

**What it means for you.**

- The column changes the tool, not the result, as long as effort is high.
- The light columns cost more reps and more discomfort, and they build less maximal strength.
- That is why the Ideal column matters more from Phase III (Strength).

**Why the cap is 30 reps (practice rule).** The low-load studies that matched heavy-load growth used sets of roughly 25–35 reps. Past that, sets turn into tests of discomfort. Effort is also harder to judge: RIR guesses get much less accurate in sets longer than 12 reps (see Law 5).

**Grade.** Strong for the load range; Practice for the cap.

### Law 3 · Volume drives size

**Rule.** Weekly hard sets per muscle rise block by block: 8–12 in Block 1, 12–16 in Block 2, 14–16 in Block 3. V3 sets the Gain-phase numbers.

**Evidence.** The largest dose-response analysis to date pooled 67 studies and 2,058 participants. More weekly sets meant more muscle growth and more strength, with diminishing returns that were sharper for strength ([Pelland et al., 2026](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en)).

**What it means for you.** Add sets gradually, because recovery, time and your study load all set a ceiling. When time is short, keep the effort and cut the sets. V4's maintenance minimum is the floor.

**Grade.** Strong.

### Law 4 · Frequency spreads the work

**Rule.** Each muscle is trained twice a week, through Upper A and B and Lower A and B.

**Evidence.** In the same dose-response analysis, training a muscle more often consistently helped strength. For muscle growth it showed no consistent effect once weekly volume was accounted for ([Pelland et al., 2026](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en)).

**What it means for you.**

- Twice a week is mainly a way to spread the weekly sets, keep sessions to 60–75 minutes, and practise the moves.
- A missed session costs little if you make up the week's sets.
- That is why the rule is "shift the week, never stack two sessions".

**Grade.** Moderate.

### Law 5 · Progress is earned

**Rule.** Use double progression: hit the top of the rep range on every set at the target RIR, then add load or climb a rung. Restart at the bottom of the range, and log every working set.

**Evidence.**

- **Progressive overload is the backbone of every resistance-training study.** "Double progression" itself is a coaching method rather than a tested protocol.
- **RIR guesses are usable but imperfect.** On average, people underestimate how many reps they have left by about one. Guesses get more accurate near failure and in sets of 12 reps or fewer ([Halperin et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/)).

**What it means for you: calibrate.** Every 2–3 weeks, take the last set of a safe move to true failure and compare it with your guess. Safe moves are push-ups, rows, curls and calf raises. Never do this on hinges, split squats, or anything that loads the knee or back.

**Grade.** Practice, resting on moderate evidence about RIR accuracy.

### Law 6 · Rest enough

**Rule.** Rest 2–3 minutes between sets of big moves and 60–90 seconds on small ones. Pair moves that don't compete, such as a push with a pull, to save time.

**Evidence.** A Bayesian meta-analysis found a small growth advantage for resting longer than 60 seconds, and no appreciable gain beyond 90 seconds ([Singer et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/)).

**What it means for you.** Don't rush sets to chase a pump. Supersets keep sessions inside 60–75 minutes without cutting rest for the working muscle.

**Grade.** Moderate.

### Law 7 · Recover on purpose

**Rule.**

- Deload every sixth week: about half the sets, at RIR 4.
- Sleep 7.5–8.5 hours in bed.
- After a bad night, run the fatigue rule: one set fewer per exercise and one RIR more.

**Evidence.**

- **A full week off cost strength, not size.** Midway through a 9-week programme, a week off did not change leg muscle growth, but it cost some leg strength ([Coleman et al., 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)).
- **THE FRAME's deload keeps you training.** It uses the same moves with fewer sets, which is closer to the maintenance research.
- **Lower volume holds what you've built if intensity stays up.** In younger adults, strength and muscle size held for up to 32 weeks on far less volume ([Spiering et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33629972/)).

**What it means for you.**

- Deloads manage fatigue and life: holidays, study crunches, illness. They are not magic.
- Moving a deload a week or two is fine. Skipping them all is not.

**Grade.** Practice, with limited trial data.

### Law 8 · Train for the phase

**Rule.** The Gain phase is led by volume. The Strength phase uses heavier loads, planned in waves (periodised).

**Evidence.**

- **Periodising helps strength, not size.** With volume equal, periodising volume and intensity improved one-rep-max strength over non-periodised training, but did not change muscle growth ([Moesgaard et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/)).
- **Heavier loads build more one-rep-max strength.** Heavy loads also outperformed light ones for one-rep-max strength ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)).

**What it means for you.** Don't overthink periodisation while chasing size; do plan it when chasing strength. This is why the Ideal column, or a gym chosen at Gate 1, becomes more valuable from Gate 2.

**Grade.** Strong.

## 4. The three columns in practice

**Every exercise row has three versions, and the rules don't change between them.**

| Column | What it uses | Best for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bodyweight | Body, floor, wall, door frame, stairs, a sturdy chair or table | Starting today; travel weeks | Legs outgrow it fast; pulling is limited |
| Intermediary | Backpack with 2 kg packets or books, water bottles, buckets, jerrycans, towel, bedsheet in a door, inner tube | Adding load before buying kit | Anchors: a solid door that opens away from you, locked; a heavy table only |
| Ideal | Pull-up bar, bands, dumbbells, rings (gym swaps from Gate 1) | Strength work and heavy legs | Cost; loads jump in steps |

1. **Pick the hardest column you can set up today.** Mixing columns inside one session is fine.
2. **Keep sets, reps and RIR identical** whichever column you use.
3. **In the light columns, push safe moves closer to failure** (Law 1).
4. **Climb when a set passes 30 reps** (Law 2), or move to the next column.
5. **Log the column you used** on the Calendar sheet, so the weekly review can see when you outgrew one.

## 5. The effort scale

**RIR (reps in reserve) is the number of clean reps you could still have done when you stopped.** Stop at technical failure, the point where form would break, not when you physically can't move.

| RIR | What it feels like | Where THE FRAME uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4+ | Easy; reps stay smooth | Deload weeks and warm-ups |
| 3 | Steady; reps stay fast | Block 1 and new moves |
| 2 | Reps are slowing | The floor for hinges, split squats, and knee or back moves |
| 1 | The last rep is a grind | Most working sets from Block 2 |
| 0 | No clean rep left | The last set of safe moves only, and calibration sets |

| Block | Weeks | Target RIR | Hard sets per muscle per week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Foundation | 1–5 | 2–3 | 8–12 |
| Deload | 6 | 4 | About half |
| 2 · Build | 7–11 | 1–2 | 12–16 |
| Deload | 12 | 4 | About half |
| 3 · Sharpen | 13–16 | 1–2 | 14–16 |
| Peak | 17 | 2 | About two-thirds |

## 6. Realistic expectations by phase

**Muscle comes slowly and unevenly, so THE FRAME judges progress on trends, not on single readings.**

- **Average gains are modest.** Across resistance-training trials in healthy men, the average measured gain in muscle mass was about 1.5 kg ([Benito et al., 2020](https://portalcientifico.upm.es/en/ipublic/item/6112286)).
- **Individuals vary hugely.** In 585 people training the same arm programme for 12 weeks, muscle size changes ranged from −2% to +59%. One-rep-max strength gains ranged from 0% to +250% ([Hubal et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15947721/)). Your numbers will be your own.
- **The Gain rate is set by evidence.** Off-season guidance suggests gaining about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight a week for novice and intermediate lifters ([Iraki et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31247944/)). At about 75 kg, that is 0.19–0.38 kg a week. THE FRAME starts at the low end.

The table below is THE FRAME's planning projection, built from those sources. It is not a promise. Gates compare your real numbers against it.

| Phase | Days | Scale (7-day average) | Waist | Muscle, projected | What else should move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · Build | 1–119 | About level in Lane B | 2–4 cm down by Day 120 | About +2–3 kg at best | Every retest: push-ups, pull-up progress, split squats |
| Reset phase | 120–133 | Up 0.5–1 kg as glycogen and water return after the mini-cut | About level | — | Strength recovers |
| II · Gain | 134–259 | +0.19–0.25 kg a week to start | Rising no faster than 0.5 cm per two weeks | About +1.5–2.5 kg | Loads and rungs up every cycle |

**Scale noise is normal.** Daily weight can swing by 1–2 kg with water, salt, gut contents and glycogen. Creatine adds about 0.5–1.5 kg of water over its first 2–3 weeks. The 7-day average and the two-week rule (section 7) filter this out.

## 7. Control loops

**Four feedback loops keep THE FRAME on course.** Each has a sensor, a target, a rule and something you change. Nothing changes on a single reading.

| Loop | Sensor | Target | Rule | What you change | How often |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Training | Log: reps, load, RIR, pain | Top of the rep range at the target RIR | Double progression; fatigue rule | Load, rung, sets | Every session; reviewed weekly |
| Fuel | 7-day average weight; weekly waist | The lane's band in kg per week | Two weeks off-band → change by 150–200 kcal | Carbohydrate first, about one fist of ugali or rice a day | Weekly (Saturday) |
| Recovery | Sleep hours, rep trend, pain scores | 7.5–8.5 h in bed; reps steady or rising; knee pain 3/10 or less | Fatigue rule, deloads, pain rules | Sets, RIR, range of motion | Daily, reviewed weekly |
| Phase | Test battery, trends, adherence | Gate criteria | Gate rules, with a default if data is missing | Phase plan, lane, kit | Days 120, 260, 316 and 365 |

**Why two weeks.** One week's average can still be noise. Two weeks in a row outside the band is a trend worth acting on.

**Why 150–200 kcal.** A daily change of 150–200 kcal is roughly 1,050–1,400 kcal a week. That is about 0.14–0.18 kg of body tissue a week, using the rough figure of 7,700 kcal per kg of fat. It moves the trend without whiplash.

## 8. Safety framework

**Load is how tissues get stronger, and pain sets the dose.** THE FRAME trains through mild, settling discomfort and stops for anything sharp, spreading or alarming.

- **The pain-monitoring model.**
  - The source trial let pain reach 5/10 during loading. It had to settle by the next morning and not build week to week ([Silbernagel et al., 2007](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/18389)).
  - THE FRAME uses a stricter 3/10 for your knee.
  - Above 3/10, or worse the next morning, shorten the range or cut the load next session.
- **Knee.** For kneecap-joint (patellofemoral) pain, expert consensus recommends exercise therapy that combines hip and knee work ([Collins et al., 2018](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/18/1170)). V5 has the protocol.
- **Back.**
  - Lifting with a rounded lower back was not linked to developing back pain. The caveat is that the studies were mostly low quality and used light loads ([Saraceni et al., 2020](https://www.safetysolutions.net.au/content/materials-handling/article/australian-study-prompts-straight-back-thesis-rethink-999835430)).
  - Exercise with education cut the risk of a new episode in prevention trials ([Steffens et al., 2016](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/44791)).
  - So THE FRAME builds back capacity gradually rather than avoiding load. Loads stay close to the body and controlled.
- **Red flags.**
  - Serious spinal problems are rare, and most single warning signs are weak on their own. A cluster still needs a clinician ([Finucane et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2020.9971)).
  - **Doctor or physio:** pain or tingling below the knee, numbness, leg weakness, night pain, or back pain with fever or unexplained weight loss.
  - **Emergency care:** numbness around the groin, or a new bladder or bowel change.
- **Hands in the cold.** If cold turns your fingers white, blue or numb, or they catch when bending, have a doctor check them. Warm your hands before grip work.

THE FRAME is not medical advice. It never diagnoses.

## 9. Myths THE FRAME doesn't build on

- **"Crunches burn belly fat."** In a 6-week trial, abdominal exercises on a steady diet did not reduce belly fat, waist size or skinfolds, though trunk endurance improved ([Vispute et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21804427/)). A meta-analysis likewise found no localised fat loss from training a body part ([Human Movement review](https://hummov.awf.wroc.pl/A-proposed-model-to-test-the-hypothesis-of-exercise-induced-localized-fat-reduction,143162,0,2.html)). "Sharper" comes from the mini-cut and total energy balance. The trunk work is for your back.
- **"Light weights only tone."** Light loads grow muscle about as well as heavy ones when the sets are hard ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)).
- **"Every set must go to failure."** Growth improves near failure, but strength doesn't need it, and RIR 1–3 works ([Robinson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)).
- **"More is always better."** Volume helps with diminishing returns, and faster for strength ([Pelland et al., 2026](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en)).
- **"Never round your back."** Rounding has not been shown to cause back pain. Sudden jumps in load are the real risk, so loads build gradually.
