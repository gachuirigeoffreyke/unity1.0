# Mvuke · Volume 1 · Foundation, People & Governance

## Summary

Two founders, a 55/45 split earned over four years, cash in as founder loans, and clear decision rights, all written in a founders' memo before any money moves. An advocate drafts the shareholders' agreement when the company forms in 2027; this volume is research, not legal advice.

## Founders and roles

| Founder | Role | Time | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner | Operations, sourcing, products, compliance, customer care, pop-ups; later the tea bar | Full-time | Daily operations, sourcing, products and compliance, within the budget |
| Mjenzi | Planning and playbooks, brand, design and photography, online store, financial model; the link to Planties and the group | Part-time, after Planties | Brand, technology, the online store and the financial model |

Mjenzi also co-founds Planties, so any deal between the two businesses follows the related-party rules in Volume 11.

## Ownership

Split: 55% partner, 45% Mjenzi. The partner works full-time; Mjenzi brings planning, brand, the online store and the group link, which would otherwise cost money. Vesting: shares are earned monthly over 4 years, with a 1-year cliff: anyone leaving in the first 12 months keeps none. The split is reviewed at month 12. Cash: goes in as founder loans and is repaid before profits are shared, so equity reflects work. Consistency: if Planties uses a different split for the same roles, match it, since both companies are meant to join one holding company. Investors: none at first; later at holding company level (Volume 11).

## Decision rights

| Decision | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Daily operations, sourcing, products and compliance, within the budget | Partner |
| Brand, technology, the online store, the financial model | Mjenzi |
| Spending above an agreed limit, borrowing, new product lines, any health wording, hiring, leases, new shareholders or investors, joining the holding company | Both |

Deadlocks: each founder writes down their position, then waits 7 days. Next, a named neutral adviser (a mentor or the accountant) mediates. If still stuck, nothing changes. No shotgun buy-out clause this early, because it favours whoever has more cash.

## Leavers and protections

Good leaver (illness, mutual agreement): keeps earned shares or sells them at fair value. Bad leaver (fraud, serious breach, competing): sells at cost. The company owns all brand, recipe and technology work. Reasonable non-compete and non-poaching terms. First refusal: shares on sale are offered to the other founder first.
