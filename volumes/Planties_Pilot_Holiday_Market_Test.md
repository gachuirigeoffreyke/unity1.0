# Planties · Pilot · Holiday Market Test

## Summary

The festive season doubles as a pre-launch market survey: sell existing stock to real buyers from 16 November to 24 December 2026, survey everyone who enquires, and carry the numbers into the January launch. One study answers questions from six research volumes at once.

Desk research first. Prompt P1 on the Research Prompts tab gathers the prices, costs, timing and rules needed to set up the test. Run it by 20 October so prices and the spend cap can be set before 1 November.

## Timeline

Holiday market test · 4 stages, 3 peak dates Pre-orders and the survey run through the first half of November. The selling window covers Black Friday (27 Nov), corporate year-end gifting in early December, the Jamhuri Day weekend (12 Dec) and the Christmas cut-off (24 Dec). Follow-ups and the review close out by 8 January 2027, ahead of launch.

## What to sell and how

Stock. Existing plants only: pick 8–12 species that are saleable now and photograph well. Buy no new plants; spend only on pots, packaging and care cards. Offer. The three launch tiers (entry desk plant, statement plant, gift set), plus a bulk gift offer for corporate year-end parties and events. Price points. Show two versions of each tier side by side, a standard pot and a premium pot, to see where buyers land.

Channels. WhatsApp Business catalogue, Instagram and TikTok, both founders' networks, and direct gift offers to 10 offices or HR teams and 5 event planners. Delivery. Two fixed delivery days a week by zone; motorbike courier only for urgent or corporate orders. Try at least two packing methods. Delivery fee. Charge it separately until 6 December, then offer free delivery above a set order value, to see which sells better. Payment. One dedicated M-Pesa number or till. If the company is not yet registered, record every shilling anyway so it can be accounted for later. Spend cap. Both founders agree a cap before 1 November for pots, packaging, ads and delivery, for example no more than 10% of the capital ceiling (KES 50,000). It sits on the Capex sheet as the holiday test line.

## Buyer survey

Everyone who enquires or buys gets five short questions by WhatsApp, and both founders share the same form with their networks. The aim is 50 responses by 24 December.

1. Where do you usually buy plants, and roughly how much do you spend on them a year?
2. What would you pay for a small desk plant in a good pot: under KES 500, KES 500– 1,000, KES 1,000–2,000, or over KES 2,000?
3. Who do you buy plants for: yourself, your home, your office, or as gifts?
4. What has stopped you buying plants before?
5. Where would you rather order: WhatsApp, Instagram, a website, or in person?

Corporate and event contacts get two more: who decides on staff and client gifts, and when is the year-end gift budget set? Answers feed R1.1, R1.2, R7.1 and R7.4.
